## 1. From cohomology to an exponent and a generator count

Let $L$ be totally imaginary, $d=[L:\mathbb Q]$, $D=|\Delta_L|$, and $S$ the places above the prime $p$. Assume $L$ contains $\mu_p$ for odd $p$ and $\mu_4$ for $p=2$. Fix $e\ge1$, put $i=2e$, $u=i\,d$, $Y=\log(2D)$, and set
$$ A=H^2(G_{L,S},\mathbb Z_p(e+1)). $$
Here $u\ge4$ and $p\le d+1$. These are precisely the group and twist in `GeneratorCountTuples/LemmaK.lean:9–17`. The equations for $S$ and the character are in `Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean:54–74`. The quotient by the closed normal subgroup generated by inertia away from $S$ is in `ReciprocityTheory/GaloisValuations/Global.lean:128–145`.

The even-degree comparison identifies $A$ with $K_i(\mathcal O_L)\{p\}$, a finite group. Weibel, *Algebraic K-Theory of Rings of Integers in Local and Global Fields*, Theorem 73, gives this formula for totally imaginary fields, including $p=2$. Here the étale cohomology is that of $\mathcal O_L[1/p]$. Schmidt, Proposition 2.3 and its proof, identify the finite-coefficient étale and restricted-ramification Galois cohomology here. All places above $p$ have been removed. The cyclotomic action on the finite coefficient groups factors through a $p$-group under the roots-of-unity hypothesis. For compact coefficients take inverse limits of the finite coefficient groups. The finite degree-one groups satisfy Mittag–Leffler, so no derived-limit term occurs.

Let $r=\dim_{\mathbb F_p} A/pA$. Reduction embeds $A/pA$ in $H^2(G_{L,S},\mathbb F_p(e+1))$. The mod-$p$ cyclotomic character is trivial here, so this coefficient module is isomorphic to $\mu_p$. Kummer theory gives an extension of $\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal O_L[1/p])[p]$ by $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal O_L[1/p])/p$ for this $H^2$. The local invariant map embeds the Brauer term in a product of at most $d$ groups of order $p$ because $L$ has no real places. The Picard group is a quotient of the ordinary class group. Thus
$$r\log p\le\log h_L+d\log p.$$
For an explicit crude bound, Minkowski gives an integral ideal of norm at most $2^d\sqrt D$ in each ideal class. The number of ideals of norm at most $B$ is at most $B^2\zeta_L(2)\le B^2\zeta(2)^d$. Hence $h_L\le 2^{3d}D$. In particular $r\le u^4Y$. It remains to bound the exponent of $A$, since an abelian $p$-group on $r$ generators of exponent $E$ has order at most $E^r$.

## 2. Integral homology at the small primes

Put $N=2i+1\ge5$, $\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathcal O_L)$ and let $H$ be the kernel of reduction modulo $3\mathcal O_L$. Its index $I$ is at most $3^{dN^2}$. Restriction of scalars embeds $H$ in the principal level-three subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_{dN}(\mathbb Z)$, which is torsion-free. Indeed, for an element of prime order $q$ write it as $1+3^aB$ with $a\ge1$ maximal. For $q\ne3$ its $q$th power differs from 1 modulo $3^{a+1}$. For $q=3$ its cube differs from 1 modulo $3^{a+2}$. Both contradict the order. A nontrivial finite-order element would have a power of prime order.

We give a bound on $T=|H_i(H,\mathbb Z)_{\rm tors}|$. Use Ash's contractible well-rounded retract for the free lattice $\mathcal O_L^N$. Its cells have finite stabilizers. On the barycentric subdivision $H$ acts freely. Bayer-Fluckiger, Emery and Houriet, §2.3 and Corollary 5.2, give a finite set $\Phi\subset\mathcal O_L^N$ such that the minimal vectors of any normalized well-rounded form can simultaneously be moved into $\Phi$ by one element of $\Gamma$. Writing $M=|\Phi|$, their bound is
$$ M\le N^{3N^2d^2}d^{5N^3d^4}D^{9N^3d^3}. $$
Only this geometric estimate is used from their torsion argument.

Here is an explicit count that allows arbitrarily many minimal vectors on a single cell. The real space of Hermitian forms has dimension $s=(d/2)N^2$. Each equality $q_h(v)=1$ is affine linear in $h$. For a cell with set of minimal vectors $U$, choose at most $s+1$ vectors whose augmented equations span those indexed by $U$. Within the normalized forms with $q_h(x)\ge1$ for every nonzero lattice vector $x$, the chosen equalities determine the closed face cut out by $U$. Its relative interior determines the cell. Indeed any point satisfying these equalities can be joined to an interior point by a segment; on the open segment all inequalities outside $U$ are strict. Positive definiteness is preserved there and $U$ spans $L^N$.

For a flag of cells choose its lowest-dimensional member. The other minimal sets are subsets of its minimal set by the face relation. A single translate puts them all in $\Phi$. Choose an ordered list of at most $s+1$ equations for each member and pad with an empty symbol. For flags of any *fixed* length there are at most $(1+M)^{(s+1)^2}$ codes since flags have at most $s+1$ members. Each code determines at most one flag in the retract. Passing from $\Gamma$ to $H$ multiplies the orbit count by at most $I$. Thus there are at most
$$B=I(1+M)^{(s+1)^2}$$
simplices in each degree of the quotient subdivision. Its chain complex computes the integral homology of $H$. A boundary column from degree $i+1$ has Euclidean norm at most $i+2$ even if some quotient faces coincide. A maximal rank minor and Hadamard's inequality bound the torsion of the cokernel, and hence that of its subgroup $H_i$, by $(i+2)^B$. Thus $\log T\le B\log(i+2)$.

Transfer to $H$ takes a torsion homology class to torsion, and the transfer followed by projection acts as $I$. Consequently $IT$ annihilates the torsion of $H_i(\Gamma,\mathbb Z)$. This step includes primes dividing $I$.

## 3. The uniform constant

Homological stability identifies $H_i(\Gamma,\mathbb Z)$ with $H_i(\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal O_L),\mathbb Z)$ at $N=2i+1$. See Weibel, *The K-book*, IV.1.14.1, using Bass stable rank at most two, or Bayer-Fluckiger–Emery–Houriet §3.1 for this precise choice of $N$. Give $X=B\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal O_L)^+$ its connected infinite-loop-space structure (the identity component of the algebraic K-theory space). Its integral homology is stable group homology, and $\pi_iX=K_i(\mathcal O_L)$. Apply Mathew, Theorem 6.3 with connectivity parameter $m=1$. The $p$-part of the kernel of the unstable Hurewicz map is killed by $p^{i+4}$: this majorizes both $\lceil(i-1)/2\rceil+3$ for $p=2$ and $\lceil(i+2)/(2p-2)\rceil+1$ for odd $p$. The image of $A$ is torsion. Therefore $p^{i+4}IT$ kills $A$, and
$$\log |A|\le r\big((i+4)\log p+\log I+B\log(i+2)\big).$$

For the constants, $dN\le3u\le u^2$, $s+1\le u^4$, and $\log I\le u^5$. From the displayed bound on $M$, $\log(1+M)\le u^{13}Y$: use $d,N\le u^2$, $N^3d^4\le(dN)^4\le u^8$, and $\log d,\log N\le2\log u$. Consequently $\log B\le u^{23}Y$. As $Y\ge\log2$, $B\ge I$, $i+4\le 2u$, and $p\le u$, the parenthesis is at most $B u^3$. Inserting $r\le u^4Y$ gives
$$\log |A|\le u^7Y\exp(u^{23}Y)\le\exp(u^{24}Y)\le\exp(u^{150}Y).$$
For the middle inequality take logarithms and use $7\log u+\log Y\le 7u+Y\le (u^{24}-u^{23})Y$. This proves the requested inequality by a different route. The exponent 150 is retained to match the handoff. No optimal exponent is asserted.

## 4. The odd-degree rank estimate on the archive route

Write $m=k+1$ for $k$ even. `ClassCounting/HasVal.lean:45–62` defines $\psi_a(g)=1_a\otimes g$, up to simultaneous row and column reindexing. Stabilization appends identity blocks. In `TensorHomCubes/StabDirectedSystem.lean:67`, the homology is ordinary integral group homology with trivial coefficients. At each level $a^tn\ge n\ge2m+4$ it maps isomorphically to stable degree-$m$ homology. Conjugating by a permutation has no effect there.

Rationally, the stable homology Hopf algebra under block sum is the free graded-commutative algebra on $K_{>0}(\mathcal O_L)\otimes\mathbb Q$. This follows from the rational Hurewicz and structure theorems for a connected infinite loop space. Block repetition is the diagonal followed by $a$-fold addition. It acts as $a$ on primitives, hence as $a^j$ on products of $j$ primitives. Degree $m$ has only lengths $1\le j\le m$. The final recurrence on that degree is
$$\prod_{b=2}^{m}(\psi_{a,*}-a^b\,\mathrm{id}).$$
Each length $j\ge2$ is killed by its own factor. The image lies in the primitive part, including $a=1$ and the empty product $m=1$. For odd $m>1$ Borel gives primitive dimension $r_2$ since $L$ is totally imaginary. In degree one, the determinant computes $K_1$ for a number ring (Bass–Milnor–Serre) and Dirichlet gives rank $r_2-1$. Flatness of $\mathbb Q$ identifies the integral rank of the span of the image with this rational image dimension. This proves the inequality in `ClassCounting/LemmaKOfSteps.lean:11–15` for every positive integer $a$. The additional hypothesis $m<r_2$ is allowed throughout.

## 5. The second Lemma K and the review boundary

The public §19.1(K1) asks for the effective bound in §1. The Kimi packet additionally asks for the internal 21-step reconstruction and a separate rank-two Selmer assertion. Sections 1–3 bypass that internal derivation. Section 4 proves one of its inputs. The sixteen lattice assertions and related steps still belong to a faithful reconstruction of that derivation.

For a classical newform $f$ of weight $2e+1$, Kato, Theorem 14.2(1), applies with $r=e$ over the abelian field $F=\mathbb Q(\mu_p)$. Indeed $1\le e\le 2e$ and $e\ne(2e+1)/2$. The finite torsion-coefficient Selmer group has zero rational Selmer space (use the lattice exact sequence and take corank). Each Dirichlet character modulo $p$ is trivial over $F$. Restriction on rational degree-one cohomology is injective by restriction–corestriction. The finite local conditions are preserved under restriction, giving rational vanishing for all such twists of the specified modular representation.

For the literal archive target see `Formalism/SharpRestriction/ValuationFrobeniusEpsilon/UnitaryThreeIrreduciblePoints.lean:192–199`. In the same directory, `InstLocalRingSymplecticCoefficients.lean:1317–1319` identifies the abstract space with the quotient of `SelP` by coboundaries. Lines 142–186 identify continuous cocycles and the unramified condition away from $p$. At $p$, line 1423 asserts that a locally trivial cocycle belongs to `bkf`. To invoke Kato one needs the implication in a different direction: a global SelP cocycle must restrict at $p$ to a Bloch–Kato finite class. Line 1423 does not imply this. Likewise `IsRi` at line 1777 matches geometric Frobenius with Hecke data. One still needs the normalized classical newform realization for this very representation, including coefficient descent and the dual/Tate-twist convention.
